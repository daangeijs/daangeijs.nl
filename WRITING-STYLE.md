# How posts on this site should sound

A working guide for writing and reviewing posts (by me or with an assistant). Reference for the
tone: [Avian Visitors by Teddy Warner](https://theodore.net/projects/AvianVisitors/). Background reading: William Zinsser, *On Writing Well*, on clutter and plain words; "show, don't tell" (Chekhov, via Lubbock's *The Craft of Fiction*).

## The voice

- **First person, one reader.** I'm telling a friend who tinkers what I built and how it went. "I", "my garden", "you" for the reader.
- **Say what happened, in order.** What I wanted, what I tried, what broke, what I did about it. Mistakes and dead ends stay in; they're the interesting part.
- **Concrete over general.** Names, numbers, places, dates: "a Raspberry Pi 2 from 2015", "7 MB per clip", "25 November at 10:37". If a sentence would fit any project, cut it or make it specific.
- **Mix sentence lengths.** Mostly normal sentences, now and then a very short one on its own line ("Then I bought a trail camera."). Don't overdo it.
- **Plain words.** Only words I'd say out loud. Explain a technical term once, in passing, the first time it shows up.
- **Light humour, no forced jokes.** A wry aside is fine; a punchline every paragraph is not.
- **Headings are short and a bit playful** ("The junk pile", "Two models walk into a garden"), as H3. No "Introduction" or "Conclusion".
- **Endings just stop.** Say where it stands and what's next, in a few lines. No moral of the story.

## Things that make text sound generated (avoid)

- **Announcing instead of telling:** "There were two catches.", "Here's the thing:", "The catch:", "This is where it gets interesting.", "That's the point I want to make:". Just say the catch.
- **Stock contrasts:** "not X, but Y", "It's not about X, it's about Y", "X isn't the problem. Y is."
- **Tidy threes and parallel lists** in running text ("faster, cheaper and simpler"), and bullet lists where a sentence would do.
- **Bold thesis sentences** in the middle of a paragraph. Bold is for names of parts in a list, at most.
- **Colon reveals and dramatic one-liners** stacked after each other.
- **Summary closers:** "In short", "All in all", "And that's how...", "The lesson here is...".
- **Hype and filler words:** quietly, seamlessly, robust, leverage, delve, crucial, journey, game-changer, powerful, truly, simply, essentially, ultimately.
- **Em dashes.** Use a comma, a full stop or brackets.
- **Hedging stacks:** "it might perhaps be worth considering". Say it or don't.
- **Vague claims about feelings** ("I was blown away"). Show what happened instead.

## Practical

- Read it out loud before publishing. Where you stumble, rewrite.
- Numbers come from the build (logs, measurements), not from memory.
- Images get descriptive alt text; captions only when they add something.
- Open points for me go in HTML comments: `<!-- TODO Daan: ... -->`.
